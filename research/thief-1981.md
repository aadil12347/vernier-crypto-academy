## Sourced Visual & Production Decisions
- **Wet-down asphalt nights:** Mann insisted Chicago streets be soaked with water on night-exterior shoots so asphalt acted like a dark mirror catching and multiplying urban light sources [S — ignacioaguilardop.com].
- **Uncorrected tungsten + force-developed film stock:** Thorin shot tungsten-balanced stock without neutralizing urban fluorescent/streetlamp colour spikes, yielding cool emerald-slate/teal shadows, heavy contrast, inky blacks, and visible grain from forced negative development [S — ignacioaguilardop.com].
- **Clinical hardware vs thermal lance heat:** Mel Bourne's sets favor stark architectural lines—chrome, glass, steel—while Frank's procedural tools (heavy drills, oxygen-acetylene thermal lances) contrast glowing molten orange-amber sparks against cool blue-slate darkness [S — sensesofcinema.com].
- **Opening sequence grammar:** Opens in silence into electronic pulse, focusing with documentary rigor on the real physical mechanics of safe-cracking—goggles, drill specs, thermal heat, stopwatch timing—framing craftsmanship before dialogue [S].
- **US One-Sheet Poster Grammar:** Monochrome posterized portrait/subject layered directly over brilliant fiery orange-amber sparks flying from the thermal torch [S — mubi.com].
